## Most Important Rule Changes

The following are the biggest changes players will experience when moving from 8U to 10U:

- **Player pitching for the entire at-bat:** There is no coach pitch. Pitchers throw from 35 feet, and batters can walk or strike out.
- **Stealing is allowed:** Runners may leave when the pitch leaves the pitcher's hand and may advance one base per pitch.
- **Scoring from third:** A runner established at third may score on a wild pitch, passed ball, or attempted throw by the catcher.
- **Live baserunning on batted balls:** Runners may continue advancing while the batted ball remains live.
- **Overthrows matter:** Runners may generally advance on overthrows, making backup responsibilities especially important.
- **Stopping play:** Under CCGSA 10U rules, the ball becomes dead when a throw back to the pitcher passes through the pitching circle.
- **Look-back rule:** When the pitcher controls the ball in the circle, runners must promptly return to their previous base or continue to the next base.
- **Third strike:** The batter is out on strike three even if the catcher does not catch the pitch. The dropped-third-strike rule does not apply under CCGSA 10U rules.
- **Defensive alignment:** Teams may use 10 defenders, but only six may play in the infield. Outfielders must begin at least 20 feet beyond the baselines and may not enter the infield to cover a base.
- **Position limits:** A player may play a maximum of two innings at one position. A third inning is permitted if the game reaches the fifth inning.

The familiar structure remains: a maximum of five innings, five runs per inning, the entire roster bats, and every player must play at least two defensive innings.

## Priorities Before the First Game

With three practices, focus first on the skills most likely to prevent confusion, injuries, and unnecessary extra bases. Use the third practice to connect those skills in game situations:

1. Stealing, sliding, and live-ball baserunning
2. Pitcher and catcher fundamentals
3. Covering bases, backing up throws, and stopping play
4. Knowing the count, outs, and runner locations
5. Applying the rules and responsibilities in a controlled scrimmage

Delay bunting, first-and-third defensive plays, formal appeals, advanced cutoffs, and detailed infield-fly instruction until the season is underway.

## Practice 1: Baserunning and Live-Ball Fundamentals

**Objective:** Every player understands when she may leave, where she may advance, how to slide safely, and when the play ends.

| Time | Activity | Coaching emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:10 | Team introduction and five-rule overview | Pitching, leaving on release, one base per pitch, live advancement on hits, and stopping play through the circle |
| 0:10-0:20 | Throwing warm-up | Catch first, set the feet, call the target, and make an accurate throw | End on four-corners-drill |
| 0:20-0:35 | Sliding fundamentals | Bent-leg slide, hands up, chin tucked, stay low, and begin early |
| 0:35-0:55 | Leaving on release and stealing | Watch the pitcher, leave on release, run hard, and slide through the base |
| 0:55-1:10 | Passed balls and scoring from third | Read the distance of the ball, listen to the coach, and understand when the runner is established at third |
| 1:10-1:25 | Controlled baserunning scrimmage | Use runners on different bases and stop to explain live-ball and one-base-per-pitch decisions |
| 1:25-1:30 | Review | Ask players to explain when they can leave, advance, score, and stop |

**Helpful videos:**

- [Four Corners Drill](https://www.championshipproductions.com/news/2015/05/11/train-players-to-quickly-catch-and-throw/) - Catching, transferring, and moving the ball around the diamond
- [USA Softball: Beginner Baserunning](https://www.mlb.com/video/beginner-baserunning-c2522406383) - Running through first, rounding the base, and introductory baserunning mechanics
- [How to Slide in Softball and Baseball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-ZjEtdogco) - Beginner-friendly figure-four sliding progression
- [Softball Australia: Training and Practicing Sliding](https://vimeo.com/482246156) - Additional sliding instruction and practice ideas

### End-of-practice questions

- When may a runner leave the base?
- How many bases may she steal on one pitch?
- When may a runner score from third?
- What stops the play under the CCGSA rule?

## Practice 2: Defensive Organization and Pitcher-Catcher Play

**Objective:** The team can cover the correct bases, back up throws, control the ball, and handle common pitcher-catcher situations.

| Time | Activity | Coaching emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:10 | Warm-up and adapted four-corner drill | Communication, catch-turn-step-throw, and controlled one-hop throws |
| 0:10-0:25 | Position responsibilities without a ball | Who fields, who covers, and who backs up on balls to each position |
| 0:25-0:40 | Ground balls and first-base coverage | Accurate throws, first baseman target, right-field backup, and recovery after overthrows |
| 0:40-0:55 | Outfield, cutoffs, and circle control | Outfielders throw to a reachable cutoff; infielders identify the lead runner and end the play through the circle |
| 0:55-1:15 | Pitcher-catcher fundamentals | Pitching from 35 feet, setting a target, blocking, locating the ball, covering home, and deciding when not to throw |
| 1:15-1:27 | Team defense with live runners | Add a runner at first or third and practice steals, passed balls, throws, backups, and returning the ball through the circle |
| 1:27-1:30 | Review | Ask who covers, who backs up, and how the defense stops the play |

## Practice 3: Situational Softball and Game Simulation

**Objective:** Players connect the rules and fundamentals in realistic game situations before the first game.

| Time | Activity | Coaching emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:10 | Warm-up and adapted four-corner drill | Accurate throws, quick transfers, communication, and one-hop throws when appropriate |
| 0:10-0:20 | Rapid baserunning review | Leaving on release, stealing one base per pitch, sliding, passed-ball reads, and listening to coaches |
| 0:20-0:35 | Defensive situation walkthrough | Place the entire defense and walk through balls to each position, base coverage, backups, and circle control |
| 0:35-0:50 | Pitcher-catcher with runners | Practice runner on first, runner on third, passed balls, steals, covering home, and choosing whether to throw |
| 0:50-1:20 | Controlled game simulation | Use live pitching when practical, start several innings with runners on base, and stop only for important corrections |
| 1:20-1:27 | Three-out inning challenge | Let the defense play without interruption and require players to track the count, outs, and runners |
| 1:27-1:30 | First-game expectations | Review four team goals and keep the message simple and positive |

### Recommended game-simulation situations

- Bases empty with no outs
- Runner on first attempting to steal second
- Runner on second attempting to steal third
- Runner established at third reading a wild pitch or passed ball
- Runners on first and third with the runner from first stealing
- Ground ball with a force play available
- Ball hit to the outfield with runners advancing
- Overthrow at first with the defense recovering and stopping play
- Two outs with a runner on base

During the final scrimmage, avoid correcting every mistake immediately. Let some plays finish, then ask the players what happened, where the next throw should have gone, and how the play should have ended.

**Helpful videos:**

- [USA Softball: Intermediate Baserunning](https://www.mlb.com/video/intermediate-baserunning-c2522407483) - Building on basic baserunning decisions
- [Block and Recover](https://www.dugoutedge.com/drills/block-and-recover) - Catcher blocking followed by locating the ball and preparing to throw
- [Pickle Rundown Game](https://www.dugoutedge.com/drills/softball/pickle-rundown-game) - Beginner rundown mechanics and a useful in-season practice game

### Four team goals for Game 1

1. Know the count and number of outs.
2. Run hard and listen to the base coaches.
3. Back up every throw.
4. Get the ball to the circle when there is no realistic play.

## Adapted Four-Corner Drill

The standard drill uses the four bases, but many 10U players cannot consistently throw 60 feet with good mechanics. Shorten the distance rather than encouraging high, arcing throws.

### Setup

- Create a small diamond with four cones, approximately 35 to 40 feet apart.
- Divide the team into four groups, one at each cone.
- Start with one ball.
- Players throw to the next cone and follow their throw to join that line.
- Reverse direction after several clean rotations.

**Video demonstration:** [Four Corners Drill](https://www.championshipproductions.com/news/2015/05/11/train-players-to-quickly-catch-and-throw/)

### Coaching cues

- Call the receiver's name.
- Show a clear target.
- Catch first.
- Turn, step, and throw.
- Make a firm, accurate throw.
- Follow the throw.

A controlled one-hop throw is appropriate. Teach players to deliver a low, predictable hop several feet in front of the receiver rather than throwing a high rainbow.

### Progression

1. Begin at 30 to 35 feet if needed.
2. Move to 35 to 40 feet when throws are accurate.
3. Increase distance only when players maintain good mechanics.
4. Use relay players between bases when practicing on the full 60-foot field.
5. Add a second ball only after the team communicates and catches consistently.

## Sliding Mat Progression

Teach only the feet-first bent-leg or figure-four slide before the first game.

### Technique

- One leg extends toward the base.
- The other leg bends underneath in a figure-four position.
- Both hands remain raised.
- The chin stays tucked.
- The player stays low instead of jumping into the slide.
- The slide begins early enough to carry the player through the base.

### Teaching progression

1. Sit on the mat in the figure-four position.
2. Practice raising both hands and tucking the chin.
3. Move from standing into the sliding position without running.
4. Take two or three walking steps and slide.
5. Jog from 10 to 15 feet away and slide.
6. Add a base at the end of the mat.
7. Finish with a controlled run and full slide through the base.

### Simple coaching cues

- Hands up.
- Chin tucked.
- Make a figure four.
- Stay low.
- Start early.
- Slide through the base.

Do not introduce headfirst or pop-up slides during the initial lesson.

- [How to Slide in Softball and Baseball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-ZjEtdogco)
- [Softball Australia: Training and Practicing Sliding](https://vimeo.com/482246156)

## In-Season Practice Progression

After games begin, use each practice to correct issues from the previous game and introduce one new concept.

| Week | Primary focus | Secondary focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Correct first-game baserunning and coverage issues | Rundown fundamentals |
| 2 | Bunting and bunt defense | Corner and catcher responsibilities |
| 3 | First-and-third situations | Catcher decision-making |
| 4 | Fly balls, tagging up, and force plays | Infield-fly introduction |
| 5 | Cutoffs and relays | Outfield communication |
| 6 | Tournament and game situations | Appeals and rule awareness |

### In-season drill videos

- [Pickle Rundown Game](https://www.dugoutedge.com/drills/softball/pickle-rundown-game) - Use when introducing rundowns in Week 1
- [Cutoff and Relay Circuit](https://www.dugoutedge.com/drills/softball/cutoff-relay-circuit) - Use for cutoff and relay work in Week 5
- [Block, Recover, and Throw](https://www.dugoutedge.com/drills/block-recover-throw) - Use after catchers are comfortable with basic blocking

### Standard 90-minute in-season practice

- **10 minutes:** Warm-up and throwing
- **15 minutes:** Individual fundamentals
- **20 minutes:** Correct issues from the previous game
- **20 minutes:** Introduce one new team concept
- **20 minutes:** Situational scrimmage
- **5 minutes:** Review and next-game expectations

## Rule Source Order

When rules appear to conflict, apply them in this order:

1. Fall tournament modifications, when the tournament rules are in effect
2. CCGSA 10U rules
3. USA Softball rules when CCGSA has not modified the rule

---

The goal before the first game is not complete mastery of every 10U rule. After three practices, players should understand when they may run, how to slide safely, where to throw the ball, how to stop the play, and how those responsibilities fit together during a live inning. Those fundamentals will prevent most early-season confusion.
